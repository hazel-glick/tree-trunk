My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge.

Laurel Schwulst

"A website is a file or bundle of files living on a server somewhere."

"a website creator becomes both author and architect simultaneously"

Comparing a website to physical space can help one determine their intention behind a website and how they want it to be structured.

"when you put energy into a website, in turn the website helps form your own identity."

"This is why websites are so important. They allow the author to create not only works (the "objects") but also the world (the rooms, the arrangement of rooms, the architecture!)."

This makes me think back to designing my portfolio and how I was very focused on making it look nice and easy to navigate, but I like this idea of designing it with an even deeper intention that reiterates what my work is about and the personal lens that created it.

"How could a website complement what you already do rather than competing or repeating?"

"A website, or anything interactive, is inherently unfinished"

The general idea of comparing your website to whatever physical item/setting makes it easier for you to grasp it. (house, shelf, plant, garden...)

I liked the emphasis of not putting too much pressure on a website and not feeling the need to constantly update and improve it and instead follow your more natural flow of work.

A Rant About "Technology"

Ursula K. Le Guin

"technology is how a society copes with physical reality"

"Technology is the active human interface with the material world"

"One way to illustrate that most technologies are, in fact, pretty "hi," is to ask yourself of any manmade object, Do I know how to make one?"

"That's the neat thing about technologies. They're what we can learn to do."

This quote made me wonder about AI and whether or not it surpasses this, or does it fall into this because it regurgitates things humans have done.

Interesting commentary on the vagueness of the term "technology" and how everything manmade is technology and how most of it does not receive proper credit.

A Handmade Web

J. R. Carpenter

"I evoke the term 'handmade web' to refer to web pages coded by hand rather than by software”

“Handmade web pages flourished in the mid-to-late-1990s, in the brief period after the academic web and before the corporate web.”

It’s interesting to think about how the web had different eras regarding its purpose and function.

“bright, rich, personal, slow and under construction. It was a web of sudden connections and personal links.”

“The handmade web emerged at a time when print and digital enjoyed a more symbiotic relationship.”

I’m curious what this symbiotic relationship looked like.

“These are not artifacts of a dead web but rather, signposts on a map of a living web pointing to a web as it once was, a web in progress, a web in the making.”

"In the early days of the social web, there was a broad expectation that regular people might own their own identities by having their own websites, instead of being dependent on a few big sites to host their online identity."

This makes me think about my instagram profile, and if I were to convert it into my own website entirely, how would I choose to present my chosen photos differently? How would I want people to interact with them? What features would I add or remove?

“handheld devices have distanced us from the handmade web”

Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web

Parimal Satyal

“we're slowly replacing a web that empowers with one that restricts and commoditizes people.”

“we the architects of the modern web—web designers, UX designers, developers, creative directors, social media managers, data scientists, product managers, start-up people, strategists—are destroying it.”

This definitely makes me think because I’ve thought about pursuing a career in UX, but I certainly do not want to contribute to harmful web development. I took a UX class at VCU and something my professor would talk about was to design intuitive and efficient websites, but to make sure you’re doing so ethically.
To do so ethically is certainly ideal, but figuring out what that means is a whole different thing.

“we might be accomplices in the destruction of a platform that was meant to empower and bring people together”

“we are instead building a machine that surveils, subverts, manipulates, overwhelms and exploits people.”

“Today, we are so far from that initial vision of linking documents to share knowledge that it's hard to simply browse the web for information without constantly being asked to buy something”

Yep.

“Craft hyper-personalized messages to change voting behavior based on your individual personalities, and by extension, your attitudes, opinions and fears.”

This is a very scary concept that is unfortunately very real.
